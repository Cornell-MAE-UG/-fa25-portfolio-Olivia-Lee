This problem analyzes steady-state one-dimensional heat conduction through a composite plane wall consisting of two different materials. Wall A has a thickness of 30 cm and experiences uniform volumetric heat generation, while its left surface is thermally insulated, meaning no heat leaves through that boundary. Wall B, which is 10 cm thick, does not generate heat and is attached directly to wall A with no contact thermal resistance between the two materials.

The right surface of wall B is exposed to a cooling fluid at 20 °C with a convective heat transfer coefficient of 500 W/(m²·K), causing heat to leave the system through convection. Temperature sensors are placed at key locations: one at the insulated boundary between the insulation and wall A and another at the interface between walls A and B. These measured temperatures allow determination of unknown quantities such as the right surface temperature of wall B, the volumetric heat generation rate within wall A, and the thermal conductivity of wall A.

By applying steady-state heat conduction principles, energy balance, and thermal resistance analysis, the temperature distribution across the composite wall can be determined and visualized.
