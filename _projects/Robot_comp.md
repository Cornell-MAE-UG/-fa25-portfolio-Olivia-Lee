The MAE 3780 robot project focused on designing a fast and efficient autonomous robot for a block-collection competition. The team prioritized speed by using a 3:1 gear ratio and larger wheels to quickly reach the center of the arena and collect blocks before opposing robots. Expandable servo-driven arms increased the robot’s collection area, while a front bumper helped guide blocks into the robot’s perimeter. Although the team faced several manufacturing and assembly challenges, including gear alignment issues, weak arm attachments, and difficult mounting conditions, they successfully created a functional and competitive robot. Their hard-coded navigation strategy proved effective, helping the robot achieve a final record of four wins, two losses, and one tie, advancing to the round of 16. The project highlighted the importance of mechanical reliability, iterative testing, and effective design tradeoffs in engineering.
